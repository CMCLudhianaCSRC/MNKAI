# PHOTO CAPTURE SOP
## North India Dermatology Photo Data Collection Study

**Standard operating procedure for the person holding the phone.**
Version 1.0 · For nursing staff, study coordinators and clinicians · 2 hospitals · approx. 1 year · target 3,000–5,000 consented images

---

## 1. Purpose

This document tells you exactly how to take and handle the clinical skin photographs for this study. Consistency matters far more than beauty: a plain photograph that is sharp, evenly lit, correctly framed and correctly labelled is worth much more than a striking one that is blurred, glared or missing its ruler. You need no technical or photography background. Read Sections 3 and 5 once carefully, then work from the quick card (Section 12) at the bedside. If you are unsure whether an image is good enough, retake it — twenty extra seconds now saves a lost case later. If patient comfort, dignity or consent conflicts with anything here, patient care wins and the photograph is abandoned.

---

## 2. Equipment checklist — per station, per session

| # | Item | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Study smartphone | Minimum **12 MP main camera with autofocus**. Charged above 50%. |
| 2 | Lens cloth | Wipe the lens before the first photo. A fingerprint ruins every image that day. |
| 3 | 3D-printed spacer / standoff | Fixes camera-to-skin distance. Phones cannot focus closer than about **10–15 cm** on the main lens; the spacer is what makes close-ups repeatable. |
| 4 | Clip-on dermoscope | Required for the lesion / suspected-cancer arm. Contact plate clean. |
| 5 | Colour-reference card | In every close-up. Replace if stained, faded or bent. |

| 9 | Ring light or lamp (optional) | Only if used the same way every time. |

## 3. Device settings — set once per phone, re-check weekly

Fix these on the study phone and do not change them mid-study. Write them on a sticker on the back of the phone.

1. **Main lens only.** Never the ultrawide, never the small "macro" lens (often only 2 MP). If the app shows `0.5x / 1x / 2x`, stay on **`1x`**.
2. **Default photo mode — not high-resolution mode.** Turn OFF any "48 MP", "50 MP", "108 MP", "Hi-Res" or "ProRAW" toggle. The default (pixel-binned) mode is cleaner and focuses better in clinic light.
3. **No zoom, ever.** Zoom crops and softens. To make the lesion bigger, **move the phone closer** using the spacer.
4. **Grid ON** (`Settings > Camera > Grid`). It helps you centre the lesion and keep the phone square to the skin.
5. **Flash OFF** for the context and close-up shots — direct flash puts a hard white glare spot exactly over the surface detail we need. The built-in steady light *may* help in a dark corner, a deep fold, the scalp or the mouth, but only **diffused** (through the ring-light diffuser or a tissue). If you use it, use it for every image of that lesion and note it.
6. **HDR — recommended OFF, but verify on your device.** On many phones HDR shifts colour and flattens the lesion. *This is a recommendation, not an established fact:* at set-up, photograph one lesion with HDR on and once off, compare with the site clinician, then fix that setting for that phone for the whole study.
7. **Format:** JPEG or HEIF at **quality 90 or higher**. Disable any "storage saver" compression.
8. **Tap to focus on the lesion, then lock focus and exposure** (press and hold until `AE/AF LOCK` appears). Every shot.
9. **All beauty filters, skin smoothing, "AI scene enhancement" and auto-tone OFF.** They invent detail that is not there.
10. **Automatic upload to personal cloud accounts OFF** (personal Google Photos, iCloud, Samsung Cloud). **Location tagging OFF.**
11. **Never send study images through WhatsApp or any messaging app.** They re-compress the file and permanently destroy fine surface detail. Transfer as in Section 10.
12. **Record which phone was used for every image** in the log (make and model). Different phones are allowed and expected; we only need to know which one took which picture.

## 4. Room and lighting

1. Use the **same corner of the same room** each day where possible.
2. **Avoid mixed light.** Never half daylight from a window and half tube light — the colours come out wrong. Close the curtain and use room light, or move away from the window.
3. Avoid direct sunlight, and avoid standing so that **your shadow or the phone's shadow** falls across the lesion. Watch the preview; if a dark band crosses the lesion, move.
4. If a ring light is used, keep it at the same height and distance every time.
5. **Positioning:** seat or lay the patient so the skin is relaxed, flat and supported on the couch or a pillow. Never ask a patient to hold a limb up in the air — it shakes. Drape everything not being photographed.
6. Remove watches, bangles and rings from the field.

---

## 5. The shot list — per lesion

**Target: 3 images per lesion, 4 if raised or nodular.** In this order.

| Shot | What it shows | How |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Context / regional** | Where on the body the lesion is | Stand back so the anatomical landmark is obvious (whole forearm, whole back). Flash off. No ruler needed. **No face in frame.** |
| **2. Close-up** — the key image | The scientific image | Spacer flat on skin; ruler and colour card in frame; flash off; tap-to-focus on the lesion; lock focus and exposure. Lesion fills **≥50%** of the frame. |
| **3. Dermoscopic** | Surface and pigment structure | Where a dermoscope is available **and** the lesion is suspicious or pigmented. Contact plate flat, lesion centred and in focus. |
| **4. Second angle** (optional) | Height and shape | For raised, nodular or ulcerated lesions: repeat shot 2 from about 30–45° to the side. |

If the patient has several lesions, repeat the whole set **per lesion** and log each lesion separately.

---

## 6. Framing rules

1. Lesion **fills at least half the frame** in the close-up.
2. Lesion **centred** — use the grid.
3. **Whole lesion plus a margin of surrounding normal skin** all round (about a finger's width). Never crop the lesion edge.
4. Keep the **camera parallel to the skin**, not tilted. Think of the phone as a book resting flat on the surface; holding the spacer fully flat does this for you.
5. **Ruler beside the lesion, never on or overlapping it**, lying flat in the same plane.
6. **Colour card fully visible**, flat, unbent, not in shadow.
7. Aim for at least **1500 pixels across the lesion region**. Get closer with the spacer rather than zooming. For reference: HAM10000, the gold-standard dermoscopy benchmark, is released at only 600 × 450 pixels — resolution is not the bottleneck. **Focus, framing and lighting are.**

---

## 7. Quality checks BEFORE you save and move on

Open the image and check all seven. **If any one fails, delete and retake immediately** — none of this can be fixed later.

- [ ] **Sharp at 100% zoom** — pinch in on the lesion edge in the preview.
- [ ] **No motion blur** (no smearing, no doubled edges).
- [ ] **No glare** or white shiny spot over the lesion.
- [ ] **No shadow** across the lesion.
- [ ] **Colour card** fully visible and flat (close-ups).
- [ ] **Ruler** visible, flat, not covering the lesion (close-ups).
- [ ] **No face, tattoo, jewellery, name band, identifying scar, background people, or papers showing names.**

---

## 8. Special situations

- **Hair-bearing areas:** part the hair with a gloved finger or clean comb and hold it aside so the whole lesion and its margin show. Do not shave for photography.
- **Scalp:** part in a straight line; a second person to hold the hair is easier. Diffused light often helps.
- **Palms and soles:** rest the hand or foot flat and relaxed on the couch. Thick creases cast shadows — angle so they do not cross the lesion.
- **Nails:** the whole nail plus nail fold and cuticle; one straight-on and one low-angle shot for the free edge.
- **Mucosa (lip, oral, genital):** the **minimum** number of images necessary, area otherwise draped, chaperone present, consent explicitly covering that site.
- **Very large lesions:** one context shot of the whole lesion with the ruler, then a close-up of the most abnormal part.
- **Scattered or widespread disease:** one regional shot showing the distribution, plus close-ups of 2–3 representative lesions, each logged separately.
- **Darker skin — read this carefully.** Automatic exposure very often **under-exposes darker skin**, so the lesion comes out crushed, black and detail-free. Check the preview: if the lesion looks like a flat dark patch with no visible texture, **raise the exposure** (after tapping to focus, slide the brightness/sun slider **up** one or two steps) and retake. Do not go so far that surrounding skin turns blown-out white. Correcting this at the bedside is one of the most valuable things you can do for this study.

## 9. Consent and privacy at the bedside

1. **Consent is explained and signed BEFORE the camera comes out of the bag.** Never photograph first and consent afterwards.
2. Explain in the patient's own language: what will be photographed, that images are stored under a study number and not their name, that they may be used for research on skin disease in India, and that refusing changes nothing about their treatment.
3. **No faces.** If the lesion is on the face, frame only the affected region and exclude the eyes and any recognisable combination of features. If that is not possible, do not photograph.
4. **Keep tattoos, birthmarks outside the lesion, name bands and jewellery out of frame in the first place** — not "cropped out later".
5. **Never store study images on a personal phone, laptop, Google Drive or Dropbox, and never send them by WhatsApp, Telegram or email.** This is a data-protection requirement under India's DPDP Act 2023 as well as an image-quality requirement.
6. Record consent status in the log **before** entering any image.
7. If a patient withdraws consent at any time, record it and tell the coordinator the same day so the images can be removed.

---

## 10. File handling and naming

**Naming convention:** `SITE-PATIENTID-LESIONNO-SHOTTYPE-YYYYMMDD`
**Example:** `CMC-0142-L1-CLOSEUP-20260725`

- `SITE` — site code assigned by the coordinator.
- `PATIENTID` — four-digit sequential study number. **Never the hospital number, the patient's name, or initials.**
- `LESIONNO` — `L1`, `L2`, `L3`… per lesion for that patient.
- `SHOTTYPE` — `CONTEXT`, `CLOSEUP`, `DERM` or `ANGLE2`.

1. Transfer to the **approved study store at the end of every clinic session** — same day, no exceptions.
2. Transfer by **cable or the institution-approved secure upload**. Not messaging apps, not personal cloud.
3. **Keep the ORIGINAL file exactly as the camera wrote it.** Do not edit, filter, brighten, sharpen, rotate-and-resave, crop-and-resave, or screenshot. If an image needs cropping for privacy, flag it to the coordinator.
4. Verify the transferred file count against the log **before** deleting anything from the phone; then delete from the phone including its trash / "recently deleted" folder.
5. The link between patient identity and study ID lives **only** in the coordinator's separately held, access-controlled key file — never in the file name or folder.

## 11. Daily wrap-up checklist

- [ ] Every patient logged: study ID, lesion number, body site, date, **phone used**, dermoscope yes/no, consent signed yes.
- [ ] All signed consent forms filed in the secure folder.
- [ ] All images transferred to the approved store, **file count verified against the log**.
- [ ] Images deleted from the phone, including the trash folder.
- [ ] Spacer, dermoscope plate and phone wiped with alcohol.
- [ ] Phone on charge; lens wiped; rulers discarded; colour card checked for staining.
- [ ] Any problem — blurred set, withdrawal, equipment fault — reported to the coordinator the same day.

## 12. Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it matters | Do this instead |
|---|---|---|
| Zooming to get closer | Crops and softens; detail lost permanently | Move closer with the spacer; stay at `1x` |
| Using the ultrawide or 2 MP "macro" lens | Much lower quality, distorted shape | Main lens only |
| High-res 48–108 MP mode left on | Slower, more blur-prone, no benefit in clinic light | Default photo mode |
| Flash on for the close-up | Glare spot sits exactly over the lesion | Flash off; diffused light if needed |
| Sending images by WhatsApp | Re-compresses and destroys detail; also a privacy breach | Cable or approved upload only |
| No colour card in frame | Colour cannot be standardised across phones and rooms | Card in every close-up |
| Ruler lying across the lesion | Hides what we need to see | Ruler adjacent, flat, same plane |
| Phone tilted, not parallel | Distorts size and shape; ruler no longer valid | Spacer held fully flat |
| Lesion small in the corner | Too few pixels on the lesion | Centred, filling ≥50% of frame |
| Not checking the preview on dark skin | Auto-exposure under-exposes; lesion becomes a flat black patch | Raise exposure a step or two, retake |
| Half daylight, half tube light | Colour cast makes the image unusable | One consistent light source |
| Photograph taken before consent signed | Ethics violation; image must be destroyed | Consent first, always |
| Storing on a personal phone or Drive | Data-protection breach under DPDP Act 2023 | Approved study store only |
| Editing or "improving" the image | Invents detail that was never there | Keep the original untouched |
